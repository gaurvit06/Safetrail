# SafeTrail: a safety companion for treks, pilgrimages and national parks

**One line:** A tourist safety app that warns you before danger, saves your SOS when there's no signal, and lets responders act fast. All AI runs through Prune, so it costs paise per trip.

**Track:** Prune, "Ship It Lean: AI Apps on a Budget"
**Pilot routes (demo):** Kedarnath, the North East (Sikkim, Arunachal, Meghalaya), and a national park or tiger reserve

---

## 1. The problem (30 s)

- Every year, tourists and pilgrims get trapped by landslides and floods, go missing on treks, wander into restricted border or core forest zones, or get crushed in crowds.
- Three things fail at the same moment:
  1. **No warning:** tourists don't know the route is closed, the zone is restricted, or the crowd ahead is dangerous.
  2. **No signal:** large stretches of these routes have no mobile data.
  3. **No common language:** a foreign tourist or a pilgrim from another state can't explain what happened to local responders.
- Responders get late, vague, duplicate calls, and nobody knows who is stuck where.

## 2. The solution: one app with two modes

**Tourist mode**
1. **Digital Tourist Pass:** issued at registration and linked to the permit the region already issues.
   - Foreigners: visa or e-Visa number, plus the PAP/RAP permit where required.
   - Indians: e-ILP or yatra registration number, DigiLocker or passport. Aadhaar is optional and masked, never required.
   - The pass is signed, shown as a QR code, and expires with the trip.
2. **Offline safety pack:** generated by AI while the tourist still has network. It includes a route briefing, the last medical posts, altitude and weather warnings, and an emergency phrasebook in the local language. It's stored on the phone.
3. **Danger-zone alerts:** the phone vibrates when the tourist enters a red zone (landslide, closed trail, restricted border, tiger core area). These alerts run on the phone with no internet.
4. **Crowd red zones:** areas with too many people turn red on the map. A stampede warning goes to everyone heading there, with a suggested wait or another route.
5. **One-tap SOS:** the tourist picks "Injured / Lost / Stuck / Medical / Unsafe". It works offline, shows **Pending transmission**, and sends automatically when any signal returns, by SMS if mobile data is down.
6. **"Are you safe?" check-in:** after a disaster, every tourist in the area gets one push. One tap answers "I'm safe" or "I need help".
7. **Family link:** family members see only the trip status and the last check-in time, never the live location.

**Responder mode** (same app, officer login)
1. **Live map:** tourist dots, red zones, a crowd heatmap and SOS pins. The officer can **draw a new red zone** and broadcast it in seconds.
2. **Incident cards:** each SOS becomes a clear report in the officer's language. Many SOS alerts from one spot are **grouped into one incident**, for example "38 people, 3 injured, between Rambara and Linchauli".
3. **Responder copilot:** a short brief and next steps based on that route's standard procedure.
4. **Accept and timeline:** the officer taps Accept and the tourist sees "Help acknowledged, 11:42". Every step (sent, received, accepted) goes into a signed, hash-chained log that can't be quietly edited.

## 3. Rule: the AI is never in the emergency path

| Job | How it runs | Needs internet? |
|---|---|---|
| Red-zone and crowd-zone alerts | Geometry on the phone | No |
| Missed check-in | Timer on the phone | No |
| SOS saving and retry | Local queue, then SMS fallback | Only to send |
| Pass validity | Digital signature check | No |
| Safety pack, reports, translation, grouping, copilot | LLM through **Prune**, on the server | Server only |

If the AI is down or over budget, reports fall back to a fixed template. **Alerts never stop.**

## 4. How Prune is used (30% cost + 20% safety)

**Every LLM call goes through Prune, using one base URL. Provider keys stay out of the app.**

| Feature | Model | Prune feature shown |
|---|---|---|
| Offline safety pack | Small | **Cache:** same route and language means the same request, so pack #10,000 is free |
| Zone advisories in many languages | Small | **Cache:** each language is paid for once |
| SOS report and translation | Small; large only for complex cases | **Routing** to the cheapest model that works |
| SOS grouping into incidents | Large, once per cluster | **Run budget and step limit** per incident |
| Responder copilot | Small | **Spend cap** per day |
| Free-text SOS notes | Any | **Injection detection** ("ignore this alert") |
| Pass and visa numbers | Never sent in a prompt | **PII shield** |
| Every call | Any | **Signed receipts**, so cost per trip and per incident can be verified |

## 5. Connectivity: the honest answer

> "In a true dead zone nothing can send a message. So we warn people **before** they enter one, save the SOS the moment it's pressed, and send it on the first bar of signal, by SMS if data is down, or when they pass a checkpoint with Wi-Fi. The AI runs on our server, where the internet already is. The tourist's safety never depends on it."

Future work: satellite SOS on newer phones, and phone-to-phone Bluetooth relay.

## 6. Why not just call 112?

> "112 responds to emergencies. SafeTrail prevents them and gives them context. It warns before the landslide zone or the crush, works when you can't speak the language, keeps your SOS when there's no signal, and hands responders a clear, grouped report. We work alongside 112 and the police; we don't replace them."

## 7. Market

- Start with routes that already require registration or permits: Kedarnath and the Char Dham yatra, the North East permit states, and national parks.
- The same app fits Amarnath, Ladakh, Sabarimala, and big events like the Kumbh.
- **Model:** a state or park licenses it, plus a small fee per tourist. Prune keeps the AI cost per tourist in paise, which is what makes per-tourist pricing work.

## 8. Live demo plan (3 minutes, judges take part)

**Setup before judging**
- Pick a red zone that covers the judges' area. GPS indoors is only accurate to about 10–30 m, so make the zone the whole hall or corridor.
- Stick a QR "checkpoint" on a door as a backup trigger.
- Prepare a "walk simulation" route on the map in case GPS is bad.
- Put the responder map on the projector, logged in as an officer.
- Pre-load one simulated landslide area and a crowd cluster (seeded test phones or demo dots).
- Label everything as simulated: "Demo zones and dispatches are simulated."

**Run of show**

| Time | What happens | What judges see |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:20 | Story: "A pilgrim on the Kedarnath route. A landslide, no signal, no common language." | One photo, one sentence |
| 0:20–0:45 | **Judges join.** Scan the QR, get a Digital Tourist Pass, tap "Start trip". | Their own dots appear live on the projector |
| 0:45–1:05 | **Draw a red zone live** around the judges: "Landslide, route closed." | **All judges' phones vibrate** with a warning in English and Hindi |
| 1:05–1:20 | **Crowd red zone:** dots cluster (judges plus seeded dots) and the area turns red. | Stampede warning: "Heavy crowd ahead. Wait 20 min or take route B" |
| 1:20–1:50 | **Offline SOS:** one judge turns on airplane mode, taps SOS → "Injured", sees **Pending transmission**, then turns airplane mode off. | The SOS pin drops on the projector with the time and location |
| 1:50–2:10 | **SOS grouping:** 2–3 more judges press SOS. | The AI merges them into one incident card with a summary in Hindi and English |
| 2:10–2:25 | **"Are you safe?"** sent to everyone in the zone. | Judges tap "I'm safe" and a live tally appears |
| 2:25–2:40 | **Responder accepts.** | The SOS judge's phone shows "Help acknowledged, 11:42". Show the signed timeline |
| 2:40–2:50 | **Family link:** open the shared link. | Only "On trip · last check-in 2 min ago" is shown, no live location |
| 2:50–3:00 | **Prune receipt and cost slide.** | Cost for this demo session, cost per trip vs naive, and "verify signature" |

**Backup plans**
- **Bad GPS:** use the QR checkpoint or the walk simulation.
- **Bad venue Wi-Fi:** use a phone hotspot for the projector laptop.
- **Prune down:** template reports keep working. Say so. It proves the safety rule.

**Optional "Break it" round (if time allows):** judges try a replayed SOS, a forged pass QR, an impossible jump in GPS location, or "ignore this alert" in the SOS note. Each is flagged, and the attempt shows on screen.

## 9. Judge Q&A, ready answers

- **"Isn't this tracking?"** Tracking is opt-in, there's a visible Stop sharing button, and location history is deleted after the trip (for example, 7 days). The family link never shows live location.
- **"Will it work in the mountains?"** Alerts and SOS saving work offline. Sending needs any signal, SMS, or a checkpoint. We don't overclaim.
- **"Can GPS be faked?"** We flag impossible travel, replayed SOS and forged passes. We don't claim to catch all GPS spoofing.
- **"Is the report an FIR?"** No. It's an incident report draft for responders. FIRs follow police procedure.
- **"Why AI at all?"** For translation, grouping 40 SOS alerts into one picture, and personal safety packs. The safety-critical parts don't use AI at all.
- **"How much does it cost?"** Show the measured cost per trip and per emergency, with Prune's signed receipts.
